What we observed. Where the opportunity may be.

What We Observed

These observations are drawn from public sources: websites, directories, industry data, PEO regulatory filings, and known operational patterns in regional PEOs of similar size and tenure.

Post-2022 operating model has not caught up to the strategy
Three years after selling the staffing division to HireQuest, the legacy website still calls dmDickason "the only Full Service Staffing Company in El Paso." Two websites are running in parallel. The legacy ASP.NET site and the current WordPress site tell different stories about who dmDickason is today.
This usually creates handoff drag, inconsistent client quoting, and SEO leakage between two competing digital identities.
Pricing tier fragmentation
The current site advertises Starter, Essential, and Supercharged packages. The legacy site still shows Silver, Gold, and Platinum. That suggests internal sales materials, broker decks, and client-facing collateral may not be aligned to a single pricing vocabulary.
When two pricing structures coexist, the opportunity is a unified pricing playbook so every prospect and broker gets the same answer.
Small-client economics may be working against you
The site describes the average PEO client as having fewer than 15 employees. Onboarding cost in a regional PEO is mostly fixed regardless of client size. If the bottom 20% of clients are on rates from five or ten years ago, the cost to serve them likely exceeds the revenue they produce.
This pattern usually surfaces three to eight clients that need renegotiation or restructuring to become sustainable.
No visible system for scoring client profitability
Most regional PEOs at this scale are operating with the same gap: no way to answer "is this client profitable today, and where is this client headed?" in real time. The companies that are still winning in five years are the ones who can answer that question in under ten seconds.
A centralized client database that scores revenue produced versus services consumed becomes the operational backbone for every decision that follows.
Great Plains Dynamics is approaching end of life
The legacy site references Microsoft Dynamics GP as the accounting backbone. Microsoft has been sunsetting that platform. If migration has not started, the reporting infrastructure that depends on GP today will need to move within the next twelve to eighteen months.
A database layer that abstracts the accounting source is the cheapest insurance against that transition. It means reports and operational intelligence survive regardless of what happens underneath.
Three inbound channels, no attribution
Brokers and agents, referral program, and direct sales all feed into the same pipeline. Without a CRM or tracking system, channel-level profitability is invisible. Some brokers may be overpaid. Some referrals under-credited.
Channel attribution does not need to be complicated. It needs to exist.
Stale staffing content is still live
The legacy site still has active job listings for positions like Dental Assistant and Cable Installer. Those clicks are either dying at a 404 or routing to HireQuest. Either way, that is leakage nobody signed up for.
A small cleanup yields immediate clarity about where inbound traffic is actually going.
Licensure caps growth geography
PEO licensure in TX and NM is a genuine moat for the El Paso border region. But it caps the ability to follow successful clients expanding into Arizona, Nevada, or Colorado. The growth question becomes whether the next license is worth the regulatory cost.
The database is what tells you whether existing clients have already grown into states you cannot currently service.

The Broader Pattern

These observations are not unique to dmDickason. They reflect a broader pressure building across regional PEOs.

National competitors like Justworks, Rippling, and ADP TotalSource are not going to win on relationships. They will never have what Don and Martha built over five decades. But they are investing heavily in operational tooling, self-service portals, real-time dashboards, and automated compliance workflows.

The question for the next five years is not whether the relationships matter. They absolutely do. The question is whether those relationships have the operational engine underneath them to stay relevant as client expectations shift.

Second-generation transitions in family PEOs work or fail on whether the operating system can be inherited. The relationships are not transferable on day one. But the database that captures them can be.

What This Could Become

If dmDickason were to invest in operational visibility, here is what the business could look like:

Client Intelligence
Every client scored on revenue produced, services consumed, and profitability. Renegotiation candidates surfaced automatically.
Unified Pricing
One pricing structure. Every broker, every direct conversation, every renewal quoting from the same playbook.
Channel Visibility
Broker, referral, and direct channel performance visible. Commission alignment based on actual contribution.
Onboarding Tracking
Every new client moving through a structured 30-60-90 day verification cadence. No handoff drift.
Compliance Posture
Workers comp class codes, ACA ALE thresholds, multi-state nexus exposure monitored proactively.
Executive Dashboard
Don sees the three numbers that matter every Monday morning. No spreadsheet assembly required.

Questions Worth Discussing

These are not gotcha questions. They are the operational questions that, once answered, make everything downstream clearer.

  • Portfolio What is the spread between the highest- and lowest-paying client on a per-payroll basis? How many clients are on rates more than five years old?
  • Renewals What does the renewal close rate look like? Are you walking through pricing every year, or are most clients on autopilot?
  • Operations Walk me through what happens between a broker introduction and the first payroll run. Where does the timeline typically drift?
  • Systems What is the closest thing you have to a CRM today? Spreadsheet, email folder, or paper?
  • Reporting Which reports does Don look at every week or month? Which ones does he want but does not have?
  • Growth When a client wants to expand into Arizona, what happens today?

Where to Start

Not a full build. Not a giant project. A focused operational diagnostic that answers the questions above and produces a prioritized roadmap for what to tackle first.

If what you read here feels directionally right, the next step is a short conversation. No commitment. Just a discussion about where the real leverage might be.
